# Research methodology

Use this reference for issues that need more than a quick relevance check. The goal is a decision-useful issue comment, not a comprehensive market report.

## Triage

Read the issue and discussion first. A clearly low-value issue has no specific user problem, plausible beneficiary, or meaningful product outcome after reasonable context checks, or it repeats an answered proposal without new evidence. Explain the reason privately to the requester and stop. Do not treat missing metrics alone as proof of low value. Borderline issues deserve a focused investigation into the missing premise.

Identify the decision the team would make next. Examples: Is the user problem common enough to warrant discovery? Is the proposed capability technically feasible with available data? Would a financial claim require a different jurisdiction, license, or disclosure? What is the least costly way to test demand?

## Evidence search

1. Check issue links, related issues, repository docs, and existing comments so the research does not repeat known material.
2. Seek current, direct sources for each unresolved premise. Prefer official API and vendor documentation for capabilities and limits; regulator and standards-body material for rules; primary datasets or original studies for behavior and market size. Use secondary analysis to discover leads or provide context, not as the sole basis for a consequential assertion.
3. Record publication or update dates and the date accessed for time-sensitive sources. Open the source itself and verify that the cited section actually supports the claim. If a source conflicts with another, describe the disagreement and likely reason.
4. Consider at least the user need, existing alternatives, data availability and quality, operational cost, privacy or security exposure, and adoption or validation path when they bear on the issue. For a financial feature, also check relevant market, jurisdiction, data licensing, disclosures, and the risk of presenting estimates as facts. Include only factors that affect the specific proposal.

## Synthesis and comment threshold

Separate three levels: **verified fact** (directly supported), **inference** (reasoned from facts), and **open question** (not established). Explain the implication for this issue, not merely what each source says.

Before posting, ask: Would a teammate who read the issue learn something that could change prioritization, scope, a test, or a risk assessment? If not, do not comment. If yes, write one compact comment with:

- A one or two sentence finding and why it matters.
- A few evidence bullets with direct source links, dates when relevant, and clear qualification of inference.
- A practical next decision or validation step and the most important unresolved question.

Avoid generic scoring tables, speculative return-on-investment calculations, and legal or investment advice. Do not convert an uncertain regulatory point into a definitive compliance claim. No repository edits or issue metadata changes are part of this workflow.
